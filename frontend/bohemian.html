Bohemian eigenvalue explorer

Jonathan Keogh

Filed under “Writings” · 20 July 2026

A Bohemian family is the set of $n \times n$ matrices whose entries range over a fixed finite population, here $P_t = \{-1,\, t,\, 1\}$. Draw $A$ uniformly from a structural subfamily — tridiagonal, dense, unit-subdiagonal Hessenberg, or Toeplitz — and pool $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ over $10^5$–$10^6$ independent draws: the image is the $\log(1+\text{count})$ histogram of those eigenvalues on the window $|\operatorname{Re}\lambda|, |\operatorname{Im}\lambda| \le 1.15\sqrt{n} + 0.35$. Because characteristic polynomials have coefficients in a small discrete set, the real axis carries excess mass (the bright filament) and neighbourhoods of low-height algebraic numbers like $0, \pm 1, \pm i$ are depleted (the dark holes). Everything runs in your browser — characteristic polynomials by Faddeev–LeVerrier, roots by Durand–Kerner, parallelized across your CPU cores — and the density re-accumulates live as you twiddle.
