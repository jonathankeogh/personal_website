Setup

Let \(X\) be a complete metric space and suppose \(f : X \to X\) is a contraction, so that \(d(f(x), f(y)) \le q\, d(x, y)\) for some \(q \in [0, 1)\). We are interested in the behaviour of the sequence \(x_{n+1} = f(x_n)\).

$$ d(x_n, x_\star) \;\le\; \frac{q^n}{1 - q}\, d(x_1, x_0) $$

The bound says the error contracts geometrically: each step shrinks the distance to the fixed point \(x_\star\) by at least a factor of \(q\). This is the substance of the Banach fixed-point theorem, and it is surprisingly general.Stefan Banach, “Sur les opérations dans les ensembles abstraits et leur application aux équations intégrales,” Fundamenta Mathematicae 3 (1922).

A good fixed-point argument hides all of its analysis inside a single inequality.
